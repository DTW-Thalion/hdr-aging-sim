# Results — predicted vs empirical correlation structure

Pre-registered in `docs/prereg_R_test.md`, committed before any result was
computed (`1ab82a5`). Generated by `scripts/run_R_validation.py`.

**Headline: the pre-registered test does not support the compiled coupling
matrix, and a diagnostic run afterwards explains why in a way that matters more
than the test itself — the predicted correlation structure is driven almost
entirely by τ, not by `G`, and it over-predicts the observed correlations by
roughly an order of magnitude.**

---

## 1. Pre-registered results

Primary specification `Q = σ²I`. ELSA 3-axis, 9 880 within-person changes;
InCHIANTI 4-axis, 1 523 changes; four age strata each.

| | ELSA | InCHIANTI | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 sign concordance | 22/24 (0.92) | 34/48 (0.71) | pass, but uninformative — `G ≥ 0` predicts positive everywhere |
| H2 pattern `r` | +0.832 [0.727, 0.906] | +0.453 [0.228, 0.631] | pass under Q=I and Q=D, **fail under Q=D2** → Q-dependent, not claimed |
| H3 level `b` | 0.139 [0.109, 0.161] | 0.049 [0.026, 0.073] | **FAIL** — both below the pre-registered [0.188, 1.342] |
| H4 specificity | p = 0.411 | p = 0.487 | **FAIL** in both |
| H5 age invariance | pass (0.111–0.171) | **FAIL** (0.024–0.068, 2.8×) | mixed |
| H6 cohort invariance | 0.139 [0.109, 0.161] | 0.049 [0.026, 0.073] | **FAIL** — CIs disjoint, 2.8× apart |

H4 was pre-registered as decisive. It fails: the real `G` predicts the observed
correlation structure no better than a reshuffle of its own entries among its
own positions (ELSA r_real +0.832 vs null +0.826 ± 0.010; InCHIANTI +0.453 vs
+0.455 ± 0.027).

**H4 "passes" under Q=D2 (p = 0.036) and that pass is spurious.** The pattern
correlation there is −0.067: the real `G` beats a null that is merely *more*
negative. A pass on H4 while H2 fails is not evidence and is not counted.

## 2. Is the test capable of detecting anything? Yes — power is adequate

A null result is worthless without a positive control. Data were simulated from
the true `G` and the full test re-run:

| N per stratum | detection rate at p < 0.05 |
|---|---|
| 400 (≈ InCHIANTI) | 0.63 |
| 1 600 | 0.82 |
| 10 000 | 1.00 |

So the design can recover a true generating matrix at these sample sizes. The
H4 failure is a real null, not an artefact of power — though at 63% power the
single-cohort result would be weak; it is the agreement of two independent
cohorts that carries it. Note the simulation assumes the model is otherwise
correct (right τ, diagonal `Q`, Gaussian OU, no measurement error), so
real-world power is lower than tabulated.

## 3. Why H4 fails — the diagnostic that matters more than the test

Permuting the non-zero entries of `G` among the non-zero positions leaves the
predicted correlation pattern **99.7% unchanged**:

| | non-zero entries | corr(R_pred permuted, R_pred real) |
|---|---|---|
| 3-axis (I, M, F) | 4 | 0.999 ± 0.001 |
| 4-axis (I, M, N, F) | 8 | 0.997 ± 0.005 |

**The off-diagonal structure of the predicted correlation matrix is set by τ,
not by which coupling entry sits where.** That is why H2 passes while H4 fails:
the pattern agreement is real but it is agreement about timescales, not about
coupling. It also means this test — and by extension the stationary covariance
generally — **cannot identify the coupling matrix**. Any future attempt to
calibrate `G` against covariance structure will fail for the same reason.

The corollary is the useful one: *the correlation structure is a test of the τ
registry.* See §5.

## 4. The level: the model over-predicts correlations by ~18×

Not pre-registered; found in diagnosis. Reported as exploratory.

| | mean \|R\| |
|---|---|
| empirical (InCHIANTI, mean over 4 strata) | **0.037** |
| predicted, adopted parameterisation | **0.667** |

Per pair, predicted against observed:

| pair | predicted | observed |
|---|---|---|
| I–N | 0.984 | +0.104 |
| M–N | 0.915 | +0.025 |
| I–M | 0.862 | +0.026 |
| N–F | 0.427 | −0.006 |

**Measurement error cannot account for this.** Classical attenuation gives
`r_obs = r_true·√(rel₁·rel₂)`. Reproducing b = 0.049 by attenuation alone needs
a change-score reliability of ~0.002 per measure; ~0.02 for ELSA. Plausible
change-score reliabilities are 0.2–0.5. Attenuation plausibly explains a factor
of 2–5 of the gap, not 18.

The mechanism is τ separation, not coupling magnitude. With τ_N = 0.005 d and
τ_I ≈ 10 d, the N axis equilibrates ~2000× faster than the rest and becomes a
near-deterministic function of them, so the model forces R(I,N) → 1. The
observed value is 0.10.

## 5. This contradicts the N sub-channel decision, and the reason I gave for it

`data/tau_registry.json` records N as two sub-channels and assigns the fast
subsystem the **vagal** one (τ = 0.003–0.008 d), on two grounds: that it is
what the published calibration used, and that *"it is the channel the empirical
validation measures: the InCHIANTI N proxy is resting heart rate from ECG."*

**The second ground is wrong and this test exposes it.** Resting heart rate
measured once at a clinic visit is a *level*, not a recovery timescale. It does
not observe the ~4-minute vagal reactivation dynamics at all. I conflated "the
proxy is a cardiac autonomic measure" with "the proxy measures the fast vagal
channel." It does not, and the correlation structure of that very proxy is
inconsistent with τ_N = 0.005 d by an order of magnitude.

Substituting the HPA sub-channel improves the worst discrepancies:

| | R(I,N) | R(M,N) | mean \|R_pred\| | over-prediction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| adopted (N vagal, F MPS) | 0.984 | 0.915 | 0.667 | 18× |
| N → HPA (1 d) | 0.456 | 0.539 | 0.481 | 13× |
| F → mechanistic grip (33.8 d) | — | — | 0.485 | 13× |
| both | — | — | 0.328 | 9× |

No τ choice closes the gap; the best still over-predicts 9×. But the direction
is consistent and it runs against two decisions made on literature grounds —
the N sub-channel, and (weakly) the F decision, where the longer grip-strength
τ also fits better.

**This does not reverse the τ registry.** The admission criterion is about what
τ *means*, and a correlation structure cannot override a definitional argument.
But it is the first empirical evidence bearing on either decision, it points
the other way on both, and the registry's stated justification for the N choice
has to be withdrawn regardless.

## 6. What this does and does not touch

- **Does not touch the units decision.** That rests on dimensional coherence
  and the φ bound, as the pre-registration stated in advance.
- **Does not touch the three architecture-validating results** (λ_max trend, Π,
  mortality prediction). None uses `G`.
- **Does touch any future use of `G` as a quantitative prior.** H4 says the
  compiled pattern carries no detectable information about observed covariance;
  §3 says covariance could not detect it even if it did.
- **Does touch the scale question.** H3 gives b = 0.049–0.139, far below the
  φ-derived [0.188, 1.342]. Two independent routes to the level now disagree
  with each other as well as with the compiled value. The level is not merely
  uncertain — it is unidentified.
- **H5 and H6 both fail**: the level is neither age-invariant (2.8× across
  InCHIANTI strata) nor cohort-invariant (2.8× between cohorts, disjoint CIs).

## 7. Recommended next step

Stop trying to validate `G` against covariance structure; §3 shows it cannot
work. Redirect this machinery to τ, where it has real discriminating power —
the F decision changes predicted F-correlations by 8× (0.43 → 0.05) and the N
decision by 2× (0.98 → 0.46), both well above the noise floor. That is a direct
empirical test of the two registry decisions that currently rest on literature
argument alone, using data already in hand.
